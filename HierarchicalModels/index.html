The normal–normal hierarchy

Fourteen true velocities are drawn from Normal(1.5, τ²) km/s. Measurement errors are independent Gaussian with known SDs increasing from 0.25 to 1.7 km/s, multiplied by your noise setting. Membership is known and selection is ignored.

θᵢ | μ ∼ Normal(μ, τ²)       xᵢ | θᵢ ∼ Normal(θᵢ, sᵢ²)
μ ∼ Normal(0, 5²)            τ is known, not estimated

V = 1 / [1/25 + Σ 1/(τ² + sᵢ²)]
m = V Σ xᵢ/(τ² + sᵢ²)       μ | x ∼ Normal(m, V)
wᵢ = τ²/(τ² + sᵢ²)
E(θᵢ | x) = wᵢ xᵢ + (1 − wᵢ)m
Var(θᵢ | x) = τ²sᵢ²/(τ² + sᵢ²) + (1 − wᵢ)²V

The last term propagates uncertainty in μ. The individual posteriors are correlated through that shared mean. Their plotted intervals are marginal 95% credible intervals (±1.96 SD), not simultaneous 95% coverage of all fourteen stars.

Three different assumptions

Partial pooling uses the known simulated dispersion. Less precise stars have smaller wᵢ and borrow more population information. The actual shift also depends on how far a measurement lies from the inferred mean.

Complete pooling fits a separate τ = 0 model regardless of the simulated dispersion. Every star then has the same posterior mean and the same nonzero uncertainty. It does not merely draw a line through the noisy data.

No pooling uses independent flat priors, giving Normal(xᵢ, sᵢ²) posteriors. These improper priors yield proper individual posteriors here, but no population-mean posterior is inferred or reported in this mode.

Playback interpolates positions and SDs between measurements and the final inference for illustration. Intermediate frames are not sequential Bayesian posteriors, and their intervals have no claimed posterior credibility. Only the final intervals are the stated posterior intervals.
