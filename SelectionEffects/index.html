What is simulated?

There are 2,400 galaxies between 30 and 800 Mpc, drawn with uniform spatial density: distance cubed is uniform. Their independent log10(L/L₀) values follow Normal(0, 0.6²). Luminosity is in arbitrary reference units. The dashed line is the theoretical population expectation, zero, not the finite sample's exact mean.

M = −18 − 2.5 log₁₀(L/L₀)
m = M + 5 log₁₀(distance / Mpc) + 25
detected ⇔ m ≤ limiting magnitude

The orange curve is the sample mean log-luminosity in ten 80-Mpc bins. Missing objects are still in the parent population even when their points are hidden. Survey completeness is the fraction detected in this particular realisation, not a universal correction factor.

Why selection belongs in the model

p(x | detected) = S(x) p(x) / ∫ S(u) p(u) du

The denominator depends on the population model. Ignoring it and fitting only detected galaxies can misidentify selection as evolution. This illustration does not estimate a corrected luminosity function or claim that all observed trends are selection effects.

This is a sharp apparent-magnitude cut with perfect flux measurements. Cosmological geometry, evolution, extinction, K-corrections and uncertain detection probabilities are deliberately excluded.
