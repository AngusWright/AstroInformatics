What this does not imply

        
Exact addition of prior and data precisions, and monotonically decreasing posterior variance, apply to the known-variance Normal model. They are not universal properties of every conjugate family.

        
The mean weight is an exact algebraic coefficient, not a probability that the prior is correct or a universal fraction of information. All selectable priors are proper. An infinitely broad Normal prior is an improper flat-prior limit, not a proper uniform distribution over the real line.

        
Data dominate a fixed prior in these examples when sufficiently informative. Convergence to a true parameter also requires an appropriate sampling model and prior support near the truth. Data cannot rescue regions excluded by a prior. Conjugacy is a computational convenience, not a reason to misrepresent prior knowledge.
