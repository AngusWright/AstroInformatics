Try this

  1. Hold the spectrum fixed and widen the alternative prior. Does the best fit change? Does its evidence?
  2. Compare Noise alone and Clear line. A simulated noise-only spectrum can still favour the line model by chance; the presets are not guaranteed decisions.
  3. Read both predictive densities at the measured amplitude. Their ratio, not the ratio of their peaks, is BF₁₀.
  4. Try a negative amplitude. The alternative includes absorption as well as emission; there is no one-sided prior hidden in the calculation.

A small, fully specified model

Seventy independent flux measurements lie from 500 to 510 nm. Their known Gaussian noise SD is σ. The continuum is fixed at 1; the Gaussian line template g has known centre 505 nm and width 0.35 nm. Only its amplitude A is unknown.

fluxᵢ ∼ Normal(1 + A gᵢ, σ²)
H₀: A = 0                   H₁: A ∼ Normal(0, w²)
Â = Σ gᵢ(fluxᵢ − 1) / Σ gᵢ²
s = σ / √Σ gᵢ²

p(Â | H₀) = Normal(Â | 0, s²)
p(Â | H₁) = Normal(Â | 0, s² + w²)
BF₁₀ = p(Â | H₁) / p(Â | H₀)
log BF₁₀ = −½ log(1 + w²/s²) + ½(Â/s)² w²/(s² + w²)

The finite positive prior SD is explicit (0.1–10); this Gaussian prior has unbounded support but integrates to one. It is not an improper flat prior or an arbitrarily truncated Gaussian. Marginal likelihoods with an unspecified flat amplitude prior would have an undefined normalisation.

The Occam trade-off

The first log-Bayes-factor term penalises diffuse prior predictions. The second rewards a signal that the null predicted poorly. Broadening a prior does not always decrease its evidence, but an unnecessarily diffuse prior wastes predictive mass away from the observations. Evidence is not merely the best-fit likelihood with an invented parameter-count penalty.

Full-spectrum evidence is ∫ L(A) p(A) dA. Residual terms orthogonal to the line template are identical under both hypotheses and cancel exactly. For comparison, the maximum likelihood ratio has log value ½(Â/s)² and cannot penalise a broad alternative prior.

The displayed model probability is BF₁₀/(1 + BF₁₀), assuming equal prior model odds. Other model odds give other probabilities. A value rounded to 0% or 100% is not mathematical certainty; the log Bayes factor remains available.

There is no search over unknown line centres, continuum, noise or widths. Those would add parameters and change the evidence. Choose priors from pre-data knowledge rather than tuning them after inspecting this spectrum.
