Symmetric noise is not symmetric migration

True log-mass x follows a truncated exponential on [8.5, 12]. Its slope k is a natural-exponential rate per dex, not a power-law exponent in base 10. Each measurement is y = x + σz with z ∼ Normal(0,1), independently for each galaxy. Errors are unbiased in log-mass, not necessarily in linear mass.

f(x) = k exp[−k(x − L)] / [1 − exp(−k(U − L))]
f_observed(y) = ∫ from L to U f(x) Normal(y | x, σ²) dx
L = 8.5, U = 12

The orange curve evaluates this normalised convolution analytically, including both finite boundaries. No untruncated exponential approximation is used. At zero noise it equals the true density, including its sharp boundaries. Tiny tails use reflected Gaussian probabilities to avoid the R formula's possible round-off when subtracting two CDF values near one.

A declining population supplies many lower-mass candidates for upward crossings but few high-mass candidates for downward crossings. The net effect depends on slope, noise, curvature, boundaries and threshold. An individual finite realisation need not have more upward crossings.

Count conservation

measured high = true high + upward crossings − downward crossings
measured low + measured high = true low + true high = 8,000

The table includes measurements outside the true [8.5, 12] support; these are valid noisy measurements and are not clipped, rejected or silently dropped. The animation increases the error scale on cached draws; it is illustrative, not repeated independent measurement or physical mass growth.
